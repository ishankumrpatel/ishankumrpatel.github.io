Vitamin K2: Importance, Benefits, and Causes of Deficiency

23 July 2023 · Ishankumar Patel


Vitamin K2, also known as menaquinone, is a form of vitamin K, a group of fat-soluble vitamins essential for blood clotting and bone health. Unlike vitamin K1, which is primarily involved in blood clotting, vitamin K2 has specific functions related to bone and cardiovascular health.

Importance of Vitamin K2:

Bone Health: Vitamin K2 plays a crucial role in regulating calcium metabolism in the body. It activates certain proteins that help transport calcium from the bloodstream into the bones, thus contributing to bone mineralization and density. Adequate vitamin K2 levels are essential for maintaining strong and healthy bones, reducing the risk of osteoporosis and fractures.

Heart Health: Research suggests that vitamin K2 may have a protective effect on cardiovascular health. It helps inhibit the calcification of arteries by directing calcium away from arterial walls and into bones. This action may help reduce the risk of atherosclerosis (hardening of the arteries) and related cardiovascular issues.

Blood Clotting: Similar to vitamin K1, vitamin K2 is involved in the blood clotting process, ensuring proper coagulation and wound healing.

Potential Cancer Prevention: Some studies have shown that vitamin K2 may have a role in cancer prevention. It appears to inhibit the growth of cancer cells and may help reduce the risk of certain types of cancers.

Dental Health: Vitamin K2 may support dental health by promoting proper mineralization of teeth, potentially reducing the risk of tooth decay.

Causes of Vitamin K2 Deficiency:

Vitamin K2 deficiency is relatively rare, as the vitamin is produced by certain bacteria in the intestines. However, several factors can contribute to lower levels of vitamin K2:

Poor Gut Health: Conditions that affect the gut's ability to absorb nutrients, such as inflammatory bowel diseases or certain medications, may lead to reduced vitamin K2 absorption.

Low Dietary Intake: Vitamin K2 is found in certain foods, but many people may not consume enough of these sources. Fermented foods like natto (a Japanese dish), certain cheeses, and other animal products (e.g., egg yolks, meat, and dairy) are good sources of vitamin K2.

Limited Conversion from Vitamin K1: Some individuals may have difficulty converting vitamin K1 from plant-based sources into vitamin K2, leading to lower K2 levels in the body.

Antibiotic Use: Antibiotics can disrupt the gut microbiome, affecting the production and absorption of vitamin K2.

Specific Health Conditions: Certain health conditions or surgeries that affect fat absorption or bile production can impact the absorption of fat-soluble vitamins like vitamin K2.

It's important to note that vitamin K2 supplementation should be done with caution, and it's best to obtain nutrients from a well-balanced diet whenever possible. If you suspect a deficiency or are considering vitamin K2 supplementation, it's recommended to consult a healthcare professional to assess your specific needs and ensure appropriate dosing.
